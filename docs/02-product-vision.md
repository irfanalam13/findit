# Findit — Product Vision

**Status:** Initial draft

## Vision
Help communities in Nepal reconnect lost belongings with their rightful owners through a simple, searchable, and trustworthy shared lost-and-found experience.

## Target users
Confirmed at a high level: people in Nepal using a community lost-and-found platform.

Personas to validate:
- A person who has lost an item and wants to publish or find a report.
- A person who has found an item and wants to make it known.
- A person browsing reports to identify whether an item may be theirs.

Age groups, regions, institutions, languages, and accessibility needs remain unknown.

## User value
- One place to browse community lost/found reports.
- A structured way to describe an item, category, location, and date.
- Search and filters to narrow relevant reports.

The repository contains code related to these ideas; whether it works reliably must be verified.

## Proposed principles (for review)
1. Keep reporting simple and collect only necessary information.
2. Make reports easy to browse and search.
3. Avoid exposing personal contact details or sensitive information by default.
4. Define safe claiming and handover processes before launch.
5. Deliver a small MVP before optional capabilities.

These are recommendations, not approved policy.

## Positioning
Findit is currently framed as a community reporting and discovery tool, not a confirmed personal inventory tracker, marketplace, delivery service, or law-enforcement system.

## Open questions
- How can a person contact a reporter or arrange a safe handover?
- Can guests submit reports, or are accounts required?
- Are images needed, and how will they be stored?
- Who can change a report's status, and what does “resolved” mean?
- Which languages and launch regions are needed?
- How should duplicate, fraudulent, or inappropriate reports be handled?
- Should reports expire or be archived?
